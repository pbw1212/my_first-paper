\begin{abstract}
In leak audits and misinformation reviews, preserved logs often begin only after a cascade is already circulating. A late suffix omits early transmissions, and silent co-sources leave no outgoing evidence. We rank unordered candidate pairs from pre-window contacts and late arrival order with a linear-time normalizer and derive an exact silent-source posterior to bound recovery under complete traces. Across 8,498 cascades on six communication networks, a 12-person shortlist recovers at least one source in up to 94.07\% of events and both in up to 23.49\%. Joint gains track contact selectivity. Recent ties to visible participants raise joint coverage from 1.28\% to 14.68\% on a departmental network and by 0.02 percentage points on a dense corporate network where non-sources share those ties. With complete traces, posterior-optimal joint coverage averages 14.22\% on uniformly seeded, one-emitter Enron cascades. Across 1,793 mailing-list discussions in five periods, historical speaking roles outperform posting volume in every period when retrieving the root author and first distinct respondent. In coupled simulations, replacing a potentially silent co-source with the first activated recipient shifts joint coverage by tens of percentage points, showing how recovery depends on target definition.
\keywords{Temporal networks \and Source localization \and Partial observation \and \mbox{Candidate retrieval}}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In an internal information-leak audit or a coordinated misinformation review, the preserved logs often begin after a document or claim is already circulating widely. A shortlist has to cover both initiators, since identifying one active spreader can leave its partner undetected. The late suffix records the order of downstream arrivals, and historical communication records indicate who could plausibly have reached them.

Two mechanisms constrain recovery. Masking removes the transmission edges closest to initiation, leaving snapshot methods to connect visible nodes through a historical graph with multiple plausible upstream paths. An initial source can also remain silent. It contributes no successful outgoing edge, so the arrival order carries more evidence about the emitter than its partner. A \pdfcopytext{top-K}{\mbox{top-$K$}} list can retain several plausible partners alongside the emitter. We compare fixed and roster-proportional budgets to assess the effect of pool size.

We model unordered source pairs with a factorized distribution and an exact $O(\vert{}V\vert{})$ normalizer. Nested tiers capture personal activity, ties to visible participants, and temporal replay. Comparing Dept2 with the denser Company network reveals when recent ties distinguish sources from non-source candidates. To quantify ambiguity under complete observation, we derive a silent-source posterior and decompose the gap to its optimal list into emitter omission and silent-partner misranking.

Mailing-list archives provide a complementary test of participation history. We hide the root author and first distinct respondent and use the visible replies to retrieve both. Coupled simulations measure the coverage change when the first activated recipient replaces a co-source. We make three contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We derive a linear-time pair normalizer for late-suffix retrieval and compare nested feature tiers on six networks. Recent ties help most when they separate sources from other candidates.
\item An exact complete-trace posterior identifies the optimal list and separates missed emitters from misranked partners. In product-prior Enron, partner misranking accounts for the larger share of the gap.
\item Historical speaking roles improve joint coverage across five mailing-list periods. Coupled simulations show that changing the second target from a co-source to the first recipient has a larger effect than changing the sender prior.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Multi-source localization}
Classical source estimators infer a single origin from an infection snapshot via rumor centrality~\cite{shah} or effective-distance geometry~\cite{brock}. Multi-source extensions generalize this snapshot paradigm using Jordan covers to minimize worst-case distance to observed infections~\cite{zhu}, graph convolutional networks (GCNSI) to learn multi-source scores from static topology~\cite{gcnsi}, joint reconstruction of missing infections and origins (NetFill)~\cite{netfill}, and graph Bayesian optimization (BOSouL) to match simulated outbreaks to an observed snapshot~\cite{bosoul}. Because these formulations compress a cascade into a static snapshot, they discard late arrival order and struggle under prefix masking, where all visible nodes may descend from a single emitter while its silent partner leaves no topological footprint.

\subsection{Temporal network retrieval}
In temporal graphs, contact ordering strictly constrains feasible diffusion paths and arrival latencies~\cite{holme,kempe_j,koss}, while burstiness and recurring motifs shape spreading dynamics~\cite{karsai,blog,college,motifs}. Prior work has inferred origins from sparse observers, dynamic message passing, or belief propagation~\cite{altarelli,lokhov,pinto}, allocated outbreak sensors~\cite{outbreak}, reconstructed diffusion networks~\cite{netinf}, and localized single sources from partial cascades~\cite{farajtabar}. More recent temporal methods score sequential snapshots (FGSSI)~\cite{fgssi}, detect sources without fixed observers~\cite{nhtd}, and benchmark single-source temporal estimators~\cite{brack}. We evaluate snapshot, sequential, and feature-based rankers under a shared roster, prefix mask and budget.

\subsection{Label and prior shift}
Under stable class-conditional distributions, expectation maximization (EM)~\cite{saerens} and black-box confusion-matrix estimators~\cite{lipton} adapt classifiers to shifted label priors, with accuracy governed by calibration and identifiability~\cite{alex,garg,guo}. In late-suffix source retrieval, each class is an unordered pair drawn prior to cascade simulation, and cascade eligibility $E$ filters on outbreak size and mask viability so that $P(S\mid E)\propto P(E\mid S)\pi(S)$. Because the selection factor $P(E\mid S)$ is unknown and varies with network structure, standard unlabeled mixture estimation over retained suffixes need not recover the underlying source prior across domains.

\section{Retrieval from Incomplete Records}
\subsection{Event construction and observation}
\paragraph{Problem Formulation.}
Let $R$ be the historical roster, $H$ the pre-window contacts, and $O$ the ordered suffix of visible arrival batches. The hidden target is a pair of initially active members $S=\{a,b\}\subset R$. The legal candidate pool $V=R\setminus\bigcup_{G\in O}G$ contains the sources, hidden joiners and inactive members of the roster. Given $(R,H,O)$ and a list budget $K$, we seek a list $L\subseteq V$ of size $\min(K,|V|)$ that maximizes $P(S\subseteq L\mid R,H,O)$. An \emph{emitter} is an initial source that transmits successfully at least once. A \emph{silent source} makes no successful transmission, even if it had opportunities to transmit.

In the main simulation, a contact from an active sender to an inactive recipient succeeds independently with probability $p=0.30$. Contacts at a common timestamp see the same pre-batch state, and newly active recipients can transmit starting at the next timestamp. We hide the longest whole-batch prefix containing at most half the new arrivals, rounded down. A cascade qualifies if at least one batch is hidden and at least three members remain visible. The scorer receives those visible batches and their order, as illustrated in \figref{f1}.

The scorer observes only pre-window contacts (dashed gray) and the ordered membership of the visible batches ($\{w\}$ and $\{x,y\}$). Both sources and all transmission edges (solid blue) are unobserved.}{f1}

\subsection{Nested feature sets and unordered-pair learning}
The shared multilayer perceptron (MLP) maps each candidate's 64 inputs to a score $s_i$ through two SiLU layers of 40 and 24 units. The inputs comprise 27 candidate descriptors, two event-level visibility variables, 29 within-event ranks, and six shared summaries (\tabref{t1}). We compute each rank as $(\mathrm{rank}_i-0.5)/|V|$, averaging ties. The two visibility ranks are constant at 0.5 and become zero after standardization. We zero excluded columns and refit each tier with training-set scaling. Absent ties and reciprocal distances to unreachable nodes are coded as zero.
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{0.94}\resulttable{tab1_features}{Nested feature sets. Each candidate descriptor contributes its value and its within-event rank. Active counts retained input columns.}{t1}{3pt}}

Multi-hop descriptors use shortest paths in the directed, unweighted graph formed by aggregating $H$. For recent contacts and replay, $\ell=0,\ldots,J-1$ indexes the $1\leq J\leq 6$ complete pre-window fortnights, starting with the most recent. In each fortnight, we draw 24 contact streams by retaining each directed contact with probability 0.30. Let $I_{\ell d}(i,v)$ indicate whether $i$ reaches $v$ through a path with strictly increasing timestamps in draw $d$, with self-reach set to zero. The lag-weighted reach is
\[
 \eta_{iv}=\frac{\sum_{\ell=0}^{J-1}2^{-\ell/2}\sum_{d=1}^{24} I_{\ell d}(i,v)}
                {24\sum_{\ell=0}^{J-1}2^{-\ell/2}}.
\]
With $\bar\eta_v$ the mean over $i\in R$, support from $i$ to $v$ is $\eta_{iv}/(\eta_{iv}+\max(0.01,\bar\eta_v))$. We group the suffix into at most five consecutive blocks at evenly spaced batch endpoints. Each visible member's residual weight is one minus the largest support from any visible member in an earlier block. The first block has weight one.

Write $h_i=\exp(s_i)$. The model assigns mass $q(i,j)=h_i h_j/Z$ to the unordered pair $i<j$ and minimizes $\log Z-s_a-s_b$. The exact normalizer is
\begin{equation}
 Z=\sum_{i<j}h_i h_j=\tfrac12\bigg[\bigg(\sum_i h_i\bigg)^2-\sum_i h_i^2\bigg].
 \label{eq:normalizer}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:normalizer} computes $Z$ in $O(|V|)$ time. On the development pools, training evaluates the equivalent pair-logit matrix in parallel. Substituting a higher score increases every affected product, so selecting the top scores maximizes captured pair mass. Sorting takes $O(|V|\log|V|)$ time after scoring.

\paragraph{Pairwise-Interaction Ablation.}
To test factorization, we add a signed rank-eight bilinear interaction on the MLP's 24-dimensional final hidden embeddings. The pair score also includes event-weighted terms for direct historical ties and shared support, computed as the residual-weighted mean of support products. Given these overlaps, scoring all pairs and computing their inclusion marginals costs $O(|V|^2)$ for fixed embedding width. Both variants use the same loss, events, seeds, and top-12 marginal decoder.

\subsection{Historical discussion roles and list decoding}
Mailing-list archives identify early speakers through their posts, with latent diffusion sources unobserved. We test whether history and the suffix recover the root author and unique first distinct respondent within 14 days. For each role $r\in\{1,2\}$, we fit an address distribution
\[
 \phi_{ri}=\phi_r(i\mid V,O)=\frac{\exp(\theta_r^\top x_{ir})}{\sum_{u\in V}\exp(\theta_r^\top x_{ur})}.
\]
The parameters $\theta_r$ weight the role-specific feature vector $x_{ir}$ for candidate $i$. At each month boundary, resolved replies from the preceding 98 days define the roster. The \emph{Roles} features are $\log(1+x)$ transforms of 28- and 98-day post counts and counts of appearances in each role on threads whose 14-day follow-up has ended. \emph{Replies} adds mean outgoing and incoming 28-day reply counts between the candidate and visible roster members, transformed in the same way.

\emph{Role-Full} adds the mean and maximum of $\log[(n_{irv}+8b_v)/((n_{ir}+8)b_v)]$ over visible $v$. Among the $n_{ir}$ historical threads where $i$ held role $r$, $n_{irv}$ include $v$ as a later speaker. The background rate is $b_v=(n_v+0.5)/(N_{\mathrm{hist}}+1)$, where $n_v$ of the $N_{\mathrm{hist}}$ threads include $v$ as a later speaker. Both features are zero when $n_{ir}=0$. We fit each role by minimizing mean negative log likelihood over reachable training labels with penalty $\lambda\|\theta_r\|_2^2/2$, selecting $\lambda$ on validation data.

Conditioned on distinct addresses, pair $i\ne j$ has mass \mbox{$\phi_{1i}\phi_{2j}/(1-\sum_{u\in V}\phi_{1u}\phi_{2u})$}. The denominator is independent of $L$, so we optimize
\[
 F(L)=M_1(L)M_2(L)-\sum_{i\in L}\phi_{1i}\phi_{2i},
 \qquad M_r(L)=\sum_{i\in L}\phi_{ri}.
\]
We build one list from the 12 largest inclusion marginals and another by greedy insertion after starting with the highest marginal. From the better list, we repeatedly take the single-address swap that most increases $F(L)$.

\subsection{The complete-trace posterior of silent sources}
\begin{proposition}[Exact Silent-Source Posterior under Complete Trace]
\label{prop:oracle}
Let $T$ contain the full contact stream, exact join times and all successful parents under synchronous independent transmission, with persistent activity, no external activations and probability $p\in(0,1)$. Condition on the roster and fixed contact stream, with source-set prior $\pi$. Let $A$ be the successful parents who never appear as joiners and $k\geq|A|$ the known source count. Replay the observed trajectory with $A$ initially active. For each candidate $u$ outside $A$ and the joiners, activate $u$ alone in addition to $A$. Relative to the replay, let $H_u$ count newly eligible outgoing attempts and $B_u$ count incoming attempts that cease to be eligible. For any silent subset $D$ of $k-|A|$ such candidates, let $C(D,D)$ count directed contacts between members of $D$ within the cascade window. Then
\begin{equation}
 P(D\mid T,k)\propto\pi(A\cup D)(1-p)^{\Delta(D)},\qquad
 \Delta(D)=\sum_{u\in D}(H_u-B_u)-C(D,D).
 \label{oracle}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
A compatible trace has likelihood $p^{N_s}(1-p)^{N_f}$ for $N_s$ successful and $N_f$ failed attempts. The trace fixes $N_s$ for every candidate set. Adding $u$ to the initial state creates $H_u$ failed outgoing attempts and removes $B_u$ failed incoming attempts. The sum over $D$ includes outgoing contacts internal to $D$, whose recipients are already active. Subtracting $C(D,D)$ gives the change in failures relative to the replay seeded with $A$. Multiplying the resulting likelihood ratio by the prior gives~\eqref{oracle}.
\end{proof}

The oracle observes exact join times and all successful parents, including simultaneous parents. With $m$ eligible silent candidates and $d=k-|A|$, direct normalization sums over $\binom{m}{d}$ subsets, reducing to $m$ weights for $d=1$ once the contact counts are available.

If $k=2$ and $a$ is the only emitter, the weight $\pi(\{a,b\})(1-p)^{H_b-B_b}$ preserves prior odds among partners with equal failure-count differences. The optimal 12-name list contains $a$ and the 11 highest-mass partners with total posterior mass $U(T)$. The suffix-based list captures $Q(L,T)=\mathbf{1}\{a\in L\}\sum_{b\in L\setminus\{a\}}P(b\mid T,a)$. Its gap $U(T)-Q(L,T)$ is emitter omission when $a$ is absent and partner-ranking loss when $a$ is present.
